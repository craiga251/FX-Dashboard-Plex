TRIGGER: Look to buy in the 0.5860–0.5900 zone on a shallow intraday pullback, ideally accompanied by rejection wicks or a bullish 1H/4H close back above 0.5900.

5. Risk and correlation notes
Correlation clusters: AUD/USD and NZD/USD are highly correlated, both behaving as high‑beta, China‑ and risk‑sensitive G10 commodities currencies; taking both NZD long and AUD long is effectively a leveraged bet on the same macro theme. EUR/USD and GBP/USD are both heavily driven by European data and ECB/BoE expectations this week, so joint exposure behaves like one “Europe vs USD” trade around PMIs and UK CPI.

Bucketed risk caps: For a max 3% total portfolio risk, if you run both NZD/USD and AUD/USD longs, consider capping combined risk to 1.5–2.0% (e.g., 1.0% NZD, 0.5–1.0% AUD) rather than 1% each, to reflect their high correlation. For USD/JPY shorts plus EUR/USD longs (both partly “short USD” expressions), aim for a combined 2% risk cap unless their triggers are staggered and macro drivers diverge meaningfully.

Cross‑asset linkages: CAD exposure via USD/CAD is tightly linked to oil and Middle East headlines; a large USD/CAD short combined with AUD/NZD longs produces a sizeable “pro‑risk and pro‑commodities” tilt that should be sized accordingly. JPY shorts (or lack of JPY longs) leave the portfolio more vulnerable to a sudden risk‑off shock, so pairing USD/JPY shorts with at least one risk‑on G10 long (e.g., NZD/USD) can help balance the overall macro profile.

Event‑risk correlation: UK CPI, PMIs, and retail sales cluster GBP volatility, but they will also spill over into EUR/USD and DXY via broader European and global growth expectations. Similarly, Thursday’s PMIs and US jobless‑claims data can simultaneously affect EUR/USD, GBP/USD, and USD/JPY, so avoid having maximum size in all three around release times.
